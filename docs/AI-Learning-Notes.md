# AI60 - AI + Learning Notes

These examples describe changes actually made during the audit implementation. They are not fabricated transcripts or student research. Review them in your own words before recording.

## 1. AI asked to improve the complete submission

- Request: inspect the challenge and existing app, identify weaknesses, then implement the full audit.
- AI proposal: preserve the personalized project hook, but expose a direct workshop registration path and shorten the matcher.
- Implemented judgment: reduced six required diagnostic steps to three optional questions, removed the unsupported résumé-readiness display, and allowed solo attendance. Registration is recorded before any optional squad operation.
- Why: the challenge measures registrations. Personalization should earn attention rather than gate every visitor.

## 2. AI asked to make the dashboard credible

- AI proposal: make reach, conversion, invitation participation, overlap, and budget assumptions editable.
- Implemented judgment: separate a fixed illustrative dataset from database-only measurements. Replace the contradictory K-factor with an explicitly defined first-generation referral contribution. Share clicks are not described as delivered invitations.
- Correction: the earlier verbal audit said a 20% campus conversion scenario would yield 400. Recalculation gives 325 initial registrations and approximately 57 referrals: 382 total. The simulator and tests use the corrected arithmetic.
- Why: a transparent downside is stronger evidence of judgment than impressive seeded counters.

## 3. AI asked for a production-grade implementation

- Initial system behavior: register and squad services silently returned invented success after errors, and AI suggested urgency-based experiments in the legacy Copilot.
- Implemented judgment: reject silent success, artificial countdowns, unvalidated AI scoring, and broad project claims. Use strict errors, signed server sessions, bounded catalog matching, atomic database transactions, and example previews labeled as prepared outputs.
- Why: reliability and honest evidence are more important than simulated momentum.

## What changed from the first idea?

The initial experience emphasized a hidden AI gap, six-step assessment, builder identity, and mandatory squad creation. The final experience emphasizes a visible free workshop, achievable outputs, optional matching, valid standalone registration, and an inspectable growth model.

## If I had another 24 hours

1. Test the offer and matcher with five consenting final-year students; observe task completion rather than inventing conversion results.
2. Deploy a staging database, apply the migration after duplicate preflight, and exercise concurrent join/registration transactions.
3. Set a genuine event date, supply the organizer's contact and retention policy, and implement calendar and joining instructions.
4. Measure mobile performance on a real constrained connection and run a small, tagged offer experiment.

## What did AI suggest that was deliberately rejected?

- Compulsory referrals: penalize students who want to attend alone.
- Artificial urgency: there is no supplied event deadline to justify a timer.
- A readiness score: answers to a short form do not establish employability.
- More project categories: three scoped, demonstrable options are more credible than broad 60-minute claims.
- Silent fallback after a live write failure: this would lie about enrollment.
